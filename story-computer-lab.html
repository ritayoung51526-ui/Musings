The Computer Lab Whisper

I am, by nature, an aggressively quiet person. I don’t raise my voice in hallways, I don’t participate in cafeteria shouting matches, and my default volume is somewhere between a gentle breeze and a library murmur. People occasionally forget I am in the room until I sneeze.

生性使然，我一直是一個安靜得近乎隱形的人。在走廊裡我從不大聲說話，在學生餐廳也絕不參與任何起哄吵鬧，我說話的標準音量通常介於微風拂過與圖書館的竊竊私語之間。有時候大家甚至會徹底忘記我就坐在房間裡，直到我不小心打了個噴嚏。

Which makes what happened in the third-floor computer lab so fundamentally ruinous to my reputation.

正因如此，那天在三樓電腦室所發生的事，才對我維持多年的文靜形象造成了毀滅性的打擊。

The computer lab on a Tuesday afternoon is essentially a sacred temple of silence. Forty students were typing term papers under the watchful, terrifying eye of Mr. Vance—the lab supervisor who looked like he hadn’t smiled since 1987. The only audible sounds were the mechanical clicking of plastic keyboards and the dull whirring of computer fans.

週二下午的電腦室基本上就是一座嚴禁喧嘩的寂靜聖殿。四十名學生在電腦室管理員范斯老師那令人膽寒的嚴厲注視下敲打著學期論文。那位管理員看起來就像自1987年以來從未笑過一樣。全場唯一能聽見的聲音，只有塑膠鍵盤的清脆敲擊聲，以及主機風扇低沉的嗡嗡運轉聲。

I had just sat down at the very back row, plugged in my brand-new over-ear noise-canceling headphones, and started playing an energetic soundtrack to help me power through my history report. The thick ear cushions completely sealed off the outside world. I was cocooned in my own private universe.

我剛在最後一排坐下，插上了全新買的耳罩式降噪耳機，並放起了動感十足的背景音樂，打算藉此集中精神趕完歷史報告。厚實的耳罩將外界的聲音徹底隔絕，我整個人彷彿被包裹進了一個只屬於自己的私密宇宙。

About ten minutes in, I hit a snag with the printer settings. My friend Justin was sitting directly to my right, staring intently at a spreadsheet. I decided to lean over and ask him a quick, discreet question. In my head, I was leaning close and whispering in a polite, subtle tone.

大約過了十分鐘，我在設定印表機時遇到了問題。我的朋友賈斯汀恰好坐在我的正右手邊，全神貫注地盯著試算表。我決定湊過去，私下向他請教一個簡短的小問題。在我的想像中，我只是微微側身，以禮貌而細微的氣音低聲耳語。

Because the music was blasting directly into my eardrums, my internal volume meter was completely, catastrophically broken.

然而，由於耳機裡的音樂正對著我的耳膜狂轟濫炸，我的大腦對自身音量的感知徹底、災難性地失靈了。

“HEY JUSTIN!” I bellowed at the exact volume of a football referee announcing a penalty. “DO YOU KNOW WHY THIS STUPID MACHINE IS ASKING ME FOR GLOSS PHOTO PAPER WHEN I’M JUST PRINTING A PLAIN ESSAY?!”

「嘿，賈斯汀！」我以足球裁判吹罰點球時的最大分貝當場咆哮了出來：「你知道為什麼這台破機器非要我放亮面相片紙不可嗎？！我只是想印一篇普通的文字作業而已啊？！」

The sound hit the silent room like a thunderclap in a cathedral. The keyboard clicking stopped instantly. All of it. Thirty-nine students stopped typing at the exact same millisecond. At the very front of the room, Mr. Vance’s pen froze mid-air above his clipboard.

這聲巨響宛如大教堂裡的一記晴天霹靂，狠狠撞擊著整個死寂的房間。敲擊鍵盤的聲音在一瞬間徹底歸零。全場三十九名同學在同一毫秒停止了動作。在房間最前方的講台前，范斯老師手中的原子筆硬生生僵停在點名板上方。

Then came the collective sound of thirty-nine swivel chairs turning around in unison. Every single pair of eyes in the room locked onto me in the back corner. Justin, sitting beside me, didn’t say a word. He simply lowered his head, covered his face with both hands, and slowly slid down his chair until he was practically hidden under the desk, pretending we had never met in our lives.

緊接著，全場響起了三十九張旋轉電腦椅同時轉向後方的金屬摩擦聲。房間裡每一雙眼睛，整齊劃一地死死釘在縮在後排角落的我身上。坐在我身旁的賈斯汀一句話也沒說，只是默默低下頭，用雙手捂住臉，整個人沿著椅子緩緩向下滑，幾乎縮到了課桌底下，假裝這輩子從來不認識我。

I yanked the headphones off my ears. The silence that rushed in was thick and suffocating. Mr. Vance slowly stood up, adjusted his reading glasses, and stared across forty feet of dead quiet. “Young man,” he said with chilly precision, “if you scream at my printers one more time, you will be escorting yourself to the principal’s office.”

我一把扯下了頭上的耳機。瞬間湧入耳畔的死寂厚重得令人窒息。范斯老師緩緩站起身，推了推老花眼鏡，隔著十幾公尺死寂的空氣直視著我。「年輕人，」他語調冰冷而精準地說道，「如果你再敢對我的印表機咆哮一次，你就親自去校長辦公室報到吧。」

A wave of heat turned my face the color of a fire hydrant. I mumbled an apology so small nobody could hear it, packed my binder without printing a single page, and walked out the door with my shoes squeaking loudly against the linoleum tile.

一股滾燙的熱血頓時將我的臉頰燒得如同消防栓一樣通紅。我嘴裡喃喃擠出一句誰也聽不清的道歉，一張紙都沒印便抓起活頁夾，在一路摩擦發出尖銳聲響的塑膠地磚上低頭逃出了電腦室。

To this day, I only wear one earphone in public libraries. Once you’ve yelled at an innocent printer in front of forty silent people, you learn your lesson forever.

時至今日，在公共圖書館或安靜場合，我永遠只敢戴單邊耳機。畢竟，只要你在四十個鴉雀無聲的人面前對著一台無辜的印表機大聲咆哮過一次，那個教訓就足以讓你終生銘記。
