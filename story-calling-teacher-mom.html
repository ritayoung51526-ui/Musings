Calling the Teacher "Mom"

Short · ~4 min School Days · The Classroom Slip of the Tongue
Classroom · The Wrong Word Escapes

There is a very specific kind of humiliation reserved exclusively for children, and it usually involves your mouth saying something your brain never actually approved. I discovered this particular flavor of humiliation in the sixth grade, in front of thirty of my classmates.

有一種極其特殊的羞恥感，是專門保留給小孩子的，而且通常是你的嘴巴脫口說出了某句話，你的腦袋卻根本沒批准過。我就是在六年級那年，在三十位同學面前，親身體會到了這種獨門的羞恥滋味。

My teacher, Mrs. Patterson, was in many ways a maternal figure to our entire class. She brought snacks on test days, remembered everyone's birthdays, and had a soft, patient voice that somehow made even long division feel manageable.

我們的班導佩特森老師，在很多方面就像我們全班的媽媽一樣。她會在考試日帶點心來，記得每個人的生日，而且擁有一種柔和又有耐心的嗓音，連長除法聽起來都因此變得沒那麼可怕。

On this particular morning, I had woken up late, missed breakfast, and my mother had rushed me out the door with a hurried "have a good day, sweetheart" still ringing in my ears as I ran for the school bus.

那天早上，我睡過了頭，錯過了早餐，媽媽匆匆忙忙把我推出家門，那句急促的「乖，祝你今天過得順利」還在我耳邊迴盪，我便一路狂奔去趕校車。

I raised my hand during math class to ask a question about a homework problem I hadn't understood. Mrs. Patterson called on me with a warm smile, and every possible word in the English language was available to me at that moment. I did not choose a good one.

數學課上，我舉手想問一道我沒看懂的習題。佩特森老師帶著溫暖的笑容點名叫我發言，而在那一刻，英語裡幾乎所有的詞彙都任我挑選。而我，偏偏挑到了一個最糟糕的。

"Um, Mom, can you explain question five again?" The words left my mouth before my brain had any say in the matter. There was a beat of absolute silence, and then the entire classroom erupted into uproarious laughter.

「呃，媽，你可以再解釋一次第五題嗎？」這句話脫口而出時，我的大腦根本沒來得及發表任何意見。教室裡先是一瞬間的死寂，緊接著，全班同學便爆發出哄堂大笑。

My face turned so red I genuinely believed I could feel the heat radiating off my own cheeks. I wanted, more than anything I had ever wanted in my short eleven years of life, for the classroom floor to simply open up and swallow me whole.

我的臉瞬間漲得通紅，紅到我真心覺得自己能感受到臉頰散發出的熱氣。那一刻，在我短短十一年的人生裡，我從未如此強烈地渴望，教室的地板能夠瞬間裂開，把我整個人吞進去。

Mrs. Patterson, to her eternal credit, didn't skip a beat. She simply smiled even wider and said, "I get called that a lot, honestly—it must mean I'm doing something right," which somehow made the whole class laugh even harder, but this time, I found myself laughing along too.

佩特森老師的反應則令我永遠感激——她完全沒有猶豫，只是笑得更燦爛，說道：「老實說，我常被這樣叫，這大概表示我做對了些什麼吧。」這句話不知怎地讓全班笑得更誇張了，但這一次，我發現自己竟也跟著一起笑了出來。

For the rest of that year, "Mom" occasionally slipped out again whenever a few of us were especially tired, and it became something of a running joke rather than a source of shame. Mrs. Patterson still, to this day, sends me a birthday card every year.

在那之後的整個學年，每當我們幾個特別累的時候，「媽」這個稱呼還是偶爾會不小心溜出口，但它已經從一件丟臉的事，變成了大家心照不宣的班級笑話。而佩特森老師，直到今天，每年都還會寄生日卡片給我。
