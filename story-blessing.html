The Blessing at the Gate

Medium · ~10 min 過度保護家長 · School Gate · Mother & Daughter

I’m fifteen. Most of my friends leave school in loose groups, phones already out, laughing about something that happened fifth period. They drift toward the bus stop or the corner where someone’s older sibling is waiting with the car. No one makes a big deal out of it. Leaving school is just the ordinary end of an ordinary day.

我今年十五歲。我的大多數朋友放學時都是三三兩兩結伴走出校門，手裡早已拿出手機，有說有笑地聊著第五節課發生的趣事。他們慢悠悠地走向公車站，或在街角等待某個同學的哥哥姊姊開車來接。沒有人會大驚小怪，放學只不過是尋常一天的普通結尾。

My mom waits at the front gate every single day.

但我母親每天都必定會站在校門口等我。

She doesn’t just wait. She stands near the big tree by the entrance, arms crossed loosely, scanning the crowd until she spots me. The second I step through the gate she walks forward, places one hand gently on top of my head, and gives me a quiet blessing. It’s the same one she’s done since I was six. Soft words under her breath. A small pause. Then she smiles like everything is exactly as it should be.

她不僅僅只是等待。她站在校門口的大樹旁，雙手輕輕交疊在胸前，目光在放學的人群中仔細搜尋，直到看見我為止。當我踏出校門那一刻，她便走上前來，溫柔地將一隻手放在我的頭頂上，輕聲為我祈禱祝福。從我六歲起，她就一直在做這個相同的儀式。低聲唸幾句祝詞，微微停頓，然後露出平靜的微笑，彷彿世間的一切本該如此安詳美好。

I used to like it. When I was little it felt like a secret shield, something only the two of us shared before I walked into the noise of the school day. Now it feels like a spotlight I can’t step out of.

我小時候很喜歡這個儀式。那時它就像一面秘密護盾，是在我走入喧囂校園前只屬於我們母女倆的隱秘溫柔。但如今，它卻像一盞我怎麼也躲不開的聚光燈，讓我無處遁形。

Yesterday two girls from my English class walked past while she was doing it. One of them looked back over her shoulder and whispered something. They both started giggling—the kind of laugh that isn’t loud but still carries across the pavement. I felt heat climb up my neck and settle in my ears. I kept my face carefully blank until we reached the car. I didn’t want her to see.

昨天，我英文課的兩位同班女生剛好走過，恰好看見她撫摸著我的頭頂進行祝福。其中一個回頭看了一眼，隨即轉頭與另一個女生耳語。她們開始咯咯低笑——那種笑聲雖然不高，但在人行道上依然清晰可辨。我感覺一股熱血直衝脖子和耳根。直到坐進車裡之前，我都緊繃著臉龐，刻意保持毫無表情，因為我不想讓母親看出我的難堪。

Inside, with the doors closed and the radio low, I finally said something.

坐進車內，車門關上，廣播聲音調得很低，我終於忍不住開口了。

“Mom… can we maybe skip the blessing part? Just for a while?”

「媽……我們能不能暫時不做門口祝福這件事？哪怕只暫停一陣子？」

She kept her eyes on the road, hands steady on the wheel. For a moment she didn’t answer. Then she said, quietly, “It’s just love. Why would that embarrass you?”

她的雙眼依然注視著前方的路面，雙手沉穩地握著方向盤。有那麼一瞬間，她沒有回答。隨後，她平靜而低沉地說道：「這只是出於愛意。為什麼這會讓你感到尷尬呢？」

I didn’t have an answer that wouldn’t sound ungrateful. The words I wanted to say felt too sharp, too much like rejection. So I stayed quiet. She talked about what we were having for dinner and whether I needed new notebooks. I stared out the window and watched other kids walking freely down the sidewalk, no one waiting for them, no one placing a hand on their head in front of the entire school.

我找不到任何一句聽起來不顯得忘恩負義的回答。我想說的話過於尖銳，聽起來太像無情的拒絕。於是我選擇保持沉默。她開始聊起今晚做什麼晚餐，問我是否需要買新的筆記本。我望向車窗外，看著其他同學自由自在地走在人行道上，沒有人在校門口等候他們，更沒有人會在全校師生面前當眾將手按在他們的頭頂。

Today she did it again.

今天，她又做了一次。

I saw her from across the courtyard as soon as I stepped outside. My friends were already heading toward the bus, calling to each other, backpacks bouncing. I tried to speed up, hoping I could reach her before anyone else noticed, but the crowd moved at its own slow pace. She stepped forward the moment she saw me, the same soft smile on her face, and put her hand on my head right there in front of half the school.

我剛踏出教學樓，就穿過中庭看見了她。我的朋友們正朝著公車站走去，互相大聲呼喚著，書包在背後一顛一顛。我試圖加快步伐，希望能搶在其他人注意到之前走到她身邊，但放學的人潮挪動得太過緩慢。她一看到我，臉上依舊帶著溫柔的微笑迎上前來，就在大半個學校的注視下，將手放到了我的頭上。

I stood frozen. My backpack was only half-zipped. A group of seniors walked past on their way to the parking lot. One of them glanced over and raised his eyebrows—not mean, just curious, like he’d noticed something slightly out of place. I forced a smile that felt too tight at the edges and waited for the blessing to end.

我僵立在那裡。書包的拉鍊只拉了一半。一群正走去停車場的高年級學長走過。其中一人瞥了我一眼，微微挑起了眉毛——並沒有惡意，純粹出於好奇，彷彿看見了某件不太合時宜的畫面。我勉強擠出一個僵硬而緊繃的微笑，默默等待著祝福趕緊結束。

When we got in the car she asked if I had a good day. I said yes. She started talking about the grocery list and whether we should stop for milk. I watched the school shrink in the side mirror and wondered how long I could keep pretending this didn’t make me want to disappear into the seat.

坐進車裡，她問我今天過得好不好。我說還好。她開始聊起採買清單，詢問是否要順路買牛奶。我望著後照鏡裡那所逐漸縮小的學校，心中暗想：我到底還能假裝多久，才能掩飾自己恨不得整個人陷進座位、徹底消失的衝動？

At home I went straight to my room. I sat on the edge of the bed with my shoes still on and tried to explain it to myself in a way that didn’t make me sound like a terrible daughter. It wasn’t that I didn’t want her love. It was the opposite. I wanted it so much that the public version of it felt like too much and not enough at the same time. Like she was giving away something private in a place where people could watch and decide what it meant.

回到家，我徑直走進房間。鞋都沒脫便坐在床沿，極力在心中說服自己並不是一個不孝的女兒。不是我不渴望她的愛，恰恰相反，我太珍惜她的愛了；但這種當眾展示的形式，卻讓我同時感到窒息與失真。彷彿她正將一份本應珍藏在私密之處的深厚情感，肆意拋在公眾視線中任由他人評頭論足。

Later she knocked on my door and asked if I wanted help with homework. I said I was fine. She lingered for a second in the doorway, one hand still on the frame, like she wanted to say something else. Then she just nodded and left. I heard her footsteps move down the hall toward the kitchen.

過了一會兒，她敲了敲我的房門，問我是否需要幫忙看功課。我說不用。她在門口遲疑了片刻，一隻手依然扶在門框上，彷彿欲言又止。但最終她只是點了點頭便離開了。我聽見她的腳步聲穿過走廊，走向了廚房。

I sat there for a long time after she was gone. The house felt quiet in the particular way it does when someone is trying not to disturb you. I thought about the girls who had laughed. I thought about the senior who raised his eyebrows. I thought about my mother’s hand, warm and familiar, resting on my head like it had every day for years.

她離開後，我在床沿坐了許久。屋子裡靜得出奇，那是一種生怕打擾到你而特意放輕腳步的寂靜。我想起那兩個低聲嘲笑我的同班女生，想起挑眉的學長，也想起了母親那隻溫暖而熟悉的掌心——如同過去許多年一樣，每天按在我的頭頂。

I love her. That’s the worst part. The love is the reason it hurts. If I didn’t care, the blessing would just be something mildly annoying, easy to brush off. But I do care. I care enough that every public version of her affection feels like it’s exposing something I want to keep safe.

我很愛她。這才是最痛苦的部分。正因為深愛，這份當眾的儀式才會如此刺痛。如果我根本不在乎她，這場祝福不過是一件惱人的小事，很容易就能付之一笑。但我太在乎了。正因為在乎，她每一次在公眾場合展露的情感，都像是在將我極力想要保護的珍貴事物暴露於眾目睽睽之下。

I just wish the blessing could happen at home, behind a closed door, where no one else can see the way I still lean into her hand for half a second before I remember to pull away.

我只是由衷希望，這個祝福能發生在家裡、關起門的房間裡。在那裡，沒有人會看見我總是會情不自禁地朝她的掌心依偎半秒鐘，然後才驚覺想起要縮回來的模樣。

Tomorrow she will be at the gate again. I already know I won’t ask her to stop a second time. Not yet. Maybe not for a long time. I’ll stand there with my backpack half-zipped and my careful smile, and I’ll let her finish the words she’s been saying since I was small enough to believe they could protect me from everything.

明天，她依舊會站在校門口。我心裡明白，我不會再開口要求她停止第二次了。至少現在不會，也許很長一段時間內都不會。我依然會背著拉鍊只拉了一半的書包，掛著小心翼翼的微笑站在那裡，任由她唸完那些我從小深信能夠庇護我免受世間一切傷害的祝詞。

And then we’ll walk to the car together, and I’ll pretend the heat in my face is only from the sun.

然後，我們會一起走向車子，而我會繼續假裝自己臉頰泛起的熾熱，僅僅只是因為陽光的照曬。
