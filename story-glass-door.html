The Glass Door Incident

Short · ~3 min Everyday Clumsiness · The Spotless Glass
Office Lobby · The Glass Impact

Whoever cleans the glass doors at the community center deserves both an immediate promotion and a criminal lawsuit.

那個負責擦社區中心玻璃門的清潔員，真應該同時獲得立刻升職加薪，以及一紙故意傷害的民事訴訟。

It was a blindingly sunny Saturday afternoon. My friends and I were walking back from a run, chatting loudly about where to get burgers. I was in mid-sentence, recounting an elaborate joke, walking with the supreme, unchecked confidence of a man who believed the doorway was completely wide open.

那是一個陽光燦爛得令人眼花的週六午後。我和幾位朋友剛慢跑完往回走，正熱烈討論著待會兒去哪裡吃漢堡。我說到一半，正興高采烈地講著一個精心鋪陳的笑話，步伐邁得無比自信，滿心以為眼前的那扇大門是完全敞開著的。

The glass was so clean it did not exist in the visual spectrum. There were no stickers, no smudges, no warning decals. Just pure, transparent, unforgiving solid matter.

那塊玻璃乾淨得彷彿根本不存在於人類可見光的光譜中一樣。上面沒有任何貼紙、沒有指紋污漬，也沒有任何防撞警示標籤。只有純粹、透明且毫不留情的堅硬固體。

THUMP.

「咚——！」

The sound was loud enough that a pigeon resting on the awning took flight in terror. My forehead, nose, and open palms hit the pane simultaneously, creating a dull acoustic resonance that echoed throughout the entire tiled lobby.

那一聲悶響沉重得連遮雨棚上歇息的鴿子都嚇得振翅高飛。我的額頭、鼻尖和伸出的手掌在同一瞬間狠狠拍在了玻璃上，發出一聲沉悶的共鳴，在整個鋪滿磁磚的接待大廳裡久久迴盪。

I bounced backwards by two full steps, clutching my nose, eyes watering profusely. For two horrifying seconds, time stood still. A receptionist behind the desk stopped typing, slowly took off her glasses, and stared at me like she had just witnessed a bird crash into a skyscraper.

我被反彈得整個人向後倒退了整整兩大步，緊緊捂著鼻子，眼淚痛得止不住地往外流。在極度難熬的兩秒鐘裡，時間彷彿凝固了。服務台後面的接待員停下了打字，緩緩摘下眼鏡，神情就像親眼看見一隻麻雀筆直撞上摩天大樓玻璃幕牆一樣震驚。

My three friends did not ask if I was okay. That would require basic human sympathy. Instead, they collapsed into a heap on the pavement, wheezing with laughter so hard that one of them literally had to sit on the curb to breathe.

我的那三個朋友甚至沒有問我一句「你沒事吧」。畢竟那需要最基本的人性同情心。相反，他們當場笑倒在人行道上，笑得直喘粗氣，其中一個甚至不得不直接坐在路邊牙子上才能勉強呼吸。

To add insult to injury, the glass now displayed a perfect, grease-smudged silhouette of my forehead and the bridge of my nose, right at eye level for every visitor who arrived after me.

更讓人欲哭無淚的是，那塊原本光潔如新的玻璃門上，此刻正清晰無比地印著我額頭與鼻樑的油光輪廓，恰好位於每一位隨後進門的訪客平視的視線高度。

I reached out, pushed the actual chrome handle, and walked inside with whatever shred of dignity I had left. My nose throbbed for three hours, but the memory of that squeaking nose print will haunt me for the rest of my natural life.

我伸出手，推開了旁邊真正的金屬門把，帶著僅存的一絲尊嚴快步走進了大廳。我的鼻子足足隱隱作痛了三個小時，但留在玻璃上那個泛著油光的印記，恐怕會伴隨我的餘生久久揮之不去。
