The Train Platform Sing-Along

Short · ~4 min Commute Life · The Headphone Volume Mistake, Round Two
Subway Platform · The Accidental Concert

There is a very particular kind of joy that comes from listening to your favorite song on a mostly empty train platform late at night, when you genuinely believe no one is paying attention to you. I had forgotten, entirely, about the existence of other human ears.

在深夜幾乎空無一人的月台上，聽著自己最愛的歌曲，會湧現出一種非常特別的快樂，尤其當你真心相信根本沒有人在注意你的時候。而我，完全忘記了這個世界上還存在著其他人類的耳朵。

It was almost eleven at night. I had just left a friend's apartment, was mildly exhausted, and had one specific song stuck in my head that I simply had to play at full volume. I put my headphones in and pressed play, standing near the edge of the empty platform.

那時將近晚上十一點。我剛從朋友的公寓離開，帶著一種淡淡的疲憊感，腦海裡還單曲循環著一首非放到最大聲不可的歌。我戴上耳機按下播放鍵，站在幾乎空無一人的月台邊緣。

The chorus hit, and something in me simply could not resist. I began singing along, quietly at first, then with increasing, unbridled enthusiasm, complete with small hand gestures that I firmly believed added dramatic flair.

副歌一下，我心裡某個部分再也無法克制。我開始跟著哼唱，一開始還算小聲，接著卻愈唱愈起勁、毫無節制，還配上一些我深信能為表演增添戲劇張力的小手勢。

What I had not accounted for was that my headphones playing loud music into my own ears had made me completely deaf to my own singing volume—and I was, apparently, belting the chorus at what can only be described as full concert energy.

而我完全沒有意識到的一點是，耳機裡震耳欲聾的音樂，讓我對自己歌唱的音量徹底失去了判斷力——而我當時，顯然正以一種只能形容為全開的演唱會能量，放聲高歌著那段副歌。

A soft, muffled laugh cut through my personal concert. I turned, mid-lyric, one hand still dramatically raised in the air, to find three teenagers standing about ten feet away, filming me on their phones with barely contained delight.

一陣低沉、被壓抑住的笑聲，穿透了我的個人演唱會。我在唱到一半時轉過身，其中一隻手還誇張地高舉在半空中，卻發現三個青少年站在大約十呎外，正用手機拍著我，臉上帶著幾乎藏不住的興奮。

I froze completely, headphones still blaring, hand still raised in the air like an unfinished statue. One of them gave me a genuine, encouraging thumbs up. "Don't stop on our account," he said, grinning. "That was actually kind of great."

我整個人徹底僵住，耳機裡的音樂依然大聲放送著，那隻手也依然高舉在半空中，像一座還沒完工的雕像。其中一個孩子朝我豎起大拇指，語帶鼓勵：「別因為我們就停下來啊，」他咧嘴笑著說：「說真的，剛才那段還挺帶感的。」

I laughed, mortified but also somehow relieved, and pulled one earbud out. "How long were you watching?" I asked. "Long enough to learn the chorus," another one answered, and they all burst into laughter again, and honestly, so did I.

我笑了出來，雖然羞愧到不行，卻也不知怎地鬆了一口氣，於是拔下了一邊耳機。「你們看了多久了？」我問道。「久到我們都學會副歌了，」另一個孩子答道，接著他們又一起爆笑出聲，而說實話，我也跟著一起笑了。

My train arrived a minute later, and as the doors closed behind me, I could see them still laughing on the platform, one of them clearly replaying the video. I still listen to music on that platform, but now, I keep my mouth firmly, resolutely closed.

一分鐘後我的列車進站了，車門在我身後關上時，我還能看見他們仍在月台上笑個不停，其中一個顯然正在重播那段影片。我現在還是會在那個月台上聽音樂，只是我的嘴巴，如今總是緊緊地、堅決地閉著。
